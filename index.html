About

Structure for complex problems.

I’m a Program Manager at EKN Engineering, where I started as Program Coordinator in January 2024. I work with engineering, QA, product, and design from sprint planning through release, and I approve every production release.

I also lead our compliance work. I built our ISO 27001 and ISO 9001 programs from the ground up and led our first SOC 2 Type I and Type II audits.

I do my best work when a complex problem needs structure: a clear framework, shared priorities, and a team working toward the same goal. I’m not a software engineer, so I stay clear about what I own and what my engineering partners build.

Selected Work

What I’ve built and delivered.

01

Making prioritization clear

The problem

Requests had no consistent way into the roadmap, and much of their business value hadn’t been quantified.

What I did

I built our prioritization model and used Claude Code to build a Request Intake Tool that scores and prioritizes requests automatically before they reach the backlog. I also make the final call when priorities are disputed.

The result

The tool surfaced about $5M in previously unquantified business value for the 2026 roadmap and led to org-wide adoption of a structured process.

02

Rebuilding how we deliver

The problem

Delivery needed a more consistent rhythm and a closer link to company goals.

What I did

I rebuilt our sprint rituals from scratch, implemented Jira automations, and aligned sprint goals to OKRs. I also coordinate releases and triage critical hotfixes.

The result

Engineering output rose 25%, with full team adoption within days. A mid-cycle pivot also identified critical platform gaps and saved a $1M contract at risk.

03

Hosting more data without adding cost

The problem

I review our AWS usage bill monthly, and we needed to get more from our existing resources.

What I did

I prompted and prioritized a migration from DocumentDB to PostgreSQL and did the cost optimization research with Engineering, who executed the work.

The result

We can host an estimated 50% more data with no additional resource cost.

04

Building compliance from nothing

The problem

We needed security and quality programs where none existed, and clients needed proof.

What I did

I built our ISO 27001 and ISO 9001 programs as lead and owner, and led the company through its first SOC 2 Type I and Type II audits. I also turned client security obligations into product requirements for features including SSO, audit logs, and data archival, which the product and software teams implemented.

The result

  • ISO 27001 and ISO 9001 certification, plus passed surveillance audits with minimal findings
  • SOC 2 Type I and Type II audits passed with minimal exceptions
  • Multiple client-led cybersecurity audits passed, enabling $5M–$15M in contract revenue

05

Adopting AI responsibly

What I did

I rolled out a company-wide AI Acceptable Use Policy and an AI Management System framework aligned to ISO 42001. As a key stakeholder on our AI Steering Committee, I advise on whether AI use meets ISO and SOC standards, client needs, and regulatory guidelines. I also guide Engineering’s safe adoption of tools like Claude Code and Cursor.

Also delivered

Release coordination for three concurrent AI/ML initiatives, including a computer vision model that reached 95% accuracy and cut inspection cycle time by 20%.

Where it stands

ISO 42001 preparation is underway, and we are not certified.
